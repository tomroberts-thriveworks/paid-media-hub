How the Business Works
The core metrics and how clients find and book with Thriveworks — the funnel your paid spend feeds into.
The Two Types of Bookings
Everything at Thriveworks revolves around bookings. There are two types, and you need to know them cold:
🆕 First Bookings
The first-ever appointment booked by someone who has never booked with Thriveworks. This is the primary marketing KPI, and the number your channels are almost entirely responsible for — Melanie's team is measured on driving 20% YoY growth in First Bookings.
COE Bookings — Client calls the call center and books with an agent
Online Bookings — Client books directly through the website
🔁 Non-First Bookings
Any booking made by an existing client. This is Lifecycle Marketing's (Mason's) primary KPI, not yours directly — but every client you acquire efficiently becomes an asset in his re-engagement pool. A cheap booking that never returns is a worse outcome for the business than a slightly more expensive one that does.
Company goal: +15% YoY in Non-First Bookings (2026 OKR)

Stage 3 — First Session
This is what we define as New Client Volume (NCV)
A booking alone doesn't count. We don't make money until the client actually shows up for their appointment. The first completed session is the moment a new client becomes real revenue — and is the conversion event the entire acquisition funnel is measured against.

Stage 4 — Ongoing Care & Retention
Getting clients to come back — and keep coming back
After the first session, the goal is to keep the client engaged and attending future appointments. There are three levers for scheduling the next appointment:
Primary Lever
Clinician Scheduling
The clinician schedules future appointments directly with the client during or after their session. This is the single largest driver of future appointment scheduling.
COE Support
The call center can help clients schedule, reschedule, or manage upcoming appointments by phone.
Self-Service
Clients can book their next appointment online or manage all upcoming sessions directly in their Thriveworks account.
The Funnel Your Spend Feeds
Your job doesn't end at the click, or even at the booking. It ends at the kept appointment.

Google Ads Guide
The Marketing Ad Spend Decision Calculator
This tool tells us how much supply we have, state by state, for talk therapy and for psychiatry — supply meaning clinician/appointment capacity, not demand. It's the basis for how we tier states and structure campaigns: our highest-supply states each get their own single-state campaign ("Tier 1" in campaign-naming terms), while lower-supply states get grouped together into multi-state campaigns — that's where you'll see names like Tier 3 or Tier 4.
Open the Marketing Ad Spend Decision Calculator →
Campaign Structure — Search & PMax
Search campaigns are built off the supply tiering above — single-state campaigns for our highest-supply states, and grouped multi-state campaigns (Tier 3/Tier 4 naming) for lower-supply states, so spend concentrates where we can actually book appointments.
Exact Match campaigns target our highest commercial-intent "money keywords," grouped by state or supply cohort to concentrate spend in the highest-supply markets. I recently switched the conversion goals for the Tier 2–Tier 4 campaigns.
Service-line split: psychiatry keywords and talk therapy keywords run in separate campaign segments, because supply patterns diverge a lot by state — some states have strong psychiatry supply and weak therapy supply, or vice versa.
Performance Max runs cross-inventory, bid to a CPA/CPB target goaled on bookings, and has performed well — but it's been launched and largely left alone with no layered post-launch optimization. Bringing real PMax optimization discipline (asset groups, audience signals, search themes, asset-level performance review) is an open opportunity for you.
Demand Gen is currently off. Your call on when and how it is turned back on.
Location Assets, the Map Pack, & Call Tracking
💡
Key tip — the location asset ~93% of our spend is tied to a location asset — the vast majority of our ads run as local search formats (map pin, address, "near me" context). Keep it enabled and approved at all times; it's one of the single highest-leverage levers in the account.
The location asset report backs up that ~93% figure — we largely win in the Map Pack, not the standard top-of-page text ad positions (1–4). Finding a way to compete more in those top standard positions without sacrificing location asset performance is a real opportunity. We're getting a lot of proximity-driven ads because of this, and that's pulling in a lot of callers wanting "in person" care — but we don't have a high supply of in-person availability. So how do we use top-4 standard search positions to drive up online counseling and online psychiatry demand instead, and grow the business outside the constraints of our physical locations?
Most conversions happen on mobile, even though desktop converts at a better rate — likely because a phone call is simply easier to track end-to-end than a desktop session.
How call tracking actually works: the location asset shows a CallRail tracking number. CallRail is integrated with Google Ads, so every day we pull down call data that includes the GCLID (Google Click ID) tied to each call. We match that call to our internal data by phone number and date, and once the call is dispositioned by an agent as a scheduled intake (a new client booking), that's what fires Event 4. Event 4 is technically "calls dispositioned as scheduled intake" — closely related to, but not identical to, a true call-center booking. That distinction is worth investigating (see Next Steps).

Meta Ads Guide
Meta currently targets a handful of our highest-supply ("Tier 1") states. We previously ran Meta through an agency that performed well — since ending that relationship, both lead volume and booking volume have declined, and we need to figure out whether to fix it, rebuild it, or pause it.

Section 06
Goal Architecture & Tracking
Not all campaigns optimize toward the same conversion actions — this is the single most important technical concept to internalize before you touch a bid strategy.
Our North Star Metrics
Bookings * — the sum of Event 1 (online booking) + Event 4 (call center booking). This is our primary success metric. The asterisk denotes it's a calculated/custom metric within the platform.
CPB (Cost Per Booking) — total spend ÷ Bookings *. This is our primary efficiency metric and the lens for every optimization decision.
Bookings* = Event 1 + Event 4  |  CPB = Total Spend ÷ Bookings*
Conversion Goal Architecture
Understanding which goal type a campaign is bid to is required before interpreting its performance data — this is the single most common way new team members misread the account. (Note: this is about conversion goals, not state supply — "Tier" in this doc always refers to supply tiering, see Section 05. To avoid confusion, the two goal types below aren't called tiers.)
Bookings-Goaled Real Booking Goals
Campaigns: GGL_NBD_GSE_Geo_[State], GGL_NBD_GSE_BroadMatch_*, GGL_NBD_PMAX_*, GGL_BRD_GSE_National
Conversion goal: Event 1 & Event 4 (custom) — confirmed bookings only.
Target CPA: Historically capped around $200 — but more recently we've pushed that ceiling up to $250 in markets where we're comfortable with the LTV:CAC math. The $250 ceiling is not account-wide: it only applies in markets with a strong LTV:CAC ratio (greater than 2:1). LTV:CAC by market is visible on the Marketing Ad Spend Decision Calculator (Section 05). We need to work on defining this framework more precisely together. CPB and Bookings* data here can be taken at face value — but you need to always check what it says in-platform against what we believe to be true outside the platform.
Proxy-Goaled Proxy Goals (Legacy / Low-Volume)
Campaigns: GGL_NBD_GSE_ExactMatch_* (Tier 1 only)
Bid goal: phone leads, contacts, book-appointment proxy actions — the campaign is not bid-optimized to confirmed bookings. Event 1/4 still track and fire on these campaigns and roll up into account-level Bookings* — we just haven't pulled the trigger yet to test these on Event 1 and Event 4. This is now the only group left on proxy goals since the Tier 2–Tier 4 campaigns were recently switched over.
Target CPA: ~$30–$35. Proxy actions fire ~6–7× more often per dollar than real bookings — the CPA target reflects that frequency, not booking value. Never report a proxy-goaled campaign's own "conversions" count as its bookings — pull Bookings* at the account level instead.
⚠️
Why proxy goals exist Event 1/4 tracking is live everywhere, including on proxy-goaled campaigns — this isn't a tracking gap. Part of it is hesitation: we're afraid call volume will drop, especially on the ExactMatch campaigns, and in some cases we did test switching a campaign to Event 1/4 and spend tanked. At this point, you need to figure out what's the right call and what still needs to be tested. Where you do migrate, go one campaign at a time and expect a 1–2 week volatile learning period.
Demand Gen (GGL_NBD_DEMANDGEN_TalkTherapy) runs on a mixed goal set (Event 1, 2, 4, 5 + phone calls from website) because it won't spend on Event 1/4 alone — treat its conversion data as directional only, and it's currently the least efficient channel in the account.
HIPAA-Compliant Tracking
All tracking infrastructure must run through legal compliance. We do offline uploads with the GCLID, the conversion, and the conversion event value.
The cookie consent banner is impacting our setup with Event 1 and Event 4 — if someone doesn't accept analytics cookies, those events don't currently fire for them. That's a real gap in our booking-tracking coverage worth understanding and quantifying.
We launched an Osano-powered cookie consent banner on Thriveworks.com on May 27, 2026 for privacy compliance (CCPA/CPRA and similar state laws). Depending on the state a user is in, they see one of two experiences:
Regulation LevelBanner BehaviorStates
High-regulationOpt-in — tracking is blocked by default until the user explicitly consents (we still keep transactional bookings in AMD either way, just not the tracking/attribution)CA, CO, CT, DE, IN, IA, KY, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, RI, TN, TX, UT, VA

High-regulation states aren't a small slice either — they represent ~41.8% of our total online booking volume over the past 6 months, so this consent gap has real teeth: Google Ads is now operating with roughly 25% fewer conversion signals overall, putting an estimated 7.5% of DTC first bookings at risk until bid strategies re-baseline.

Section 10
Glossary
The terms you'll hear in every meeting — paid media specific and company-wide. Know these cold.
Paid Media & Tracking
Bookings*
Sum of Event 1 (online) + Event 4 (call center) bookings. Our primary success metric.
CPB
Cost Per Booking. Spend ÷ Bookings*. Our primary efficiency metric and optimization lens.
Event 1 / Event 4
Event 1 = online booking conversion action. Event 4 = call center booking conversion action.
NCV
New Client Volume. The count of people having their first-ever session with Thriveworks. Directly tied to First Booking, our primary marketing KPI.
Company & Business
First Booking
The first-ever appointment booked by a brand-new Thriveworks client. The primary marketing KPI. Goal: 20% YoY growth.
Non-First Booking
Any booking made by an existing client. Mason's (LCM's) primary KPI. Goal: 15% YoY growth.
COE Booking
A First Booking made when a client calls the call center and an agent books the appointment.
Online Booking
A First Booking made when a client books directly through the Thriveworks website.
LTV
Lifetime Value. $497 gross profit average (DTC total). Talk therapy: 9.97–10.46 sessions (conservative vs. with cross-sell), $462–$498 LTV. Psychiatry: 4.5–7.25 sessions, $331–$459 LTV.
CAC
Customer Acquisition Cost. $250 total (blended) — $98 marketing + $109 real estate + $43 COE. Talk therapy: $246. Psychiatry: $265.
TT
Talk Therapy. 88% of Thriveworks business.
NP / Psych
Psychiatry. 12% of business. Higher revenue per session ($159.67) and higher margin (46.1% GM) than talk therapy — but lower LTV per client ($331–$459) due to far fewer sessions per client (4.5–7.25 vs. talk therapy's 9.97–10.46).
Show Rate
% of booked appointments resulting in a completed session. Jan–Apr 2026: Calls 67.6%, Online 71.2%, Blended 68.9% (Talk 68.8% / Psych 69.5%). 2026 OKR: 84% TT / 77% psych.
PHI / HIPAA
Protected Health Information. Under HIPAA, never reference or imply a client's specific health conditions, diagnoses, or treatment details in any ad, landing page, or audience signal.
